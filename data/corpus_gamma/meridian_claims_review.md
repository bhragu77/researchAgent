# Project Kestrel: Claims Triage Automation

Meridian Insurance handles 214,000 motor claims per year through three regional
claims centres. First-notification-of-loss is captured by telephone and rekeyed
into the claims platform, and the measured rekeying error rate is 7.9 percent.

Project Kestrel proposes automated claim triage using structured intake plus
severity scoring at first notification. Modelled annual savings are GBP 940,000
against an implementation cost of GBP 510,000. Payback is estimated at 7 months.

The dominant constraint is the claims handler licensing model. Meridian's
platform charges per named handler seat, and Kestrel reduces required seats by an
estimated 26 across the three centres, which is where most of the saving arises.

Total loss claims are explicitly excluded from Kestrel's automated triage path.
Meridian's underwriting committee requires a human severity assessment on any
claim where the vehicle is declared a total loss, and that requirement is not
under review.

Storm surge and flood claims follow a separate catastrophe process that Kestrel
does not touch. During a declared catastrophe event, Meridian suspends automated
triage entirely and reverts to manual handling with surge staffing drawn from the
other two centres.
