Rules:
- "resolved_question": if the conversation above is "none", this is just the
  question, unchanged. Otherwise, rewrite it to replace every pronoun or
  implicit reference back to that conversation ("it", "that platform", "the
  first one", "what about pricing") with the actual thing it refers to, so
  the result reads naturally as a complete, standalone question that makes
  sense with NO conversation history attached at all. This is the text every
  later step (search, retrieval, the written answer) actually works from —
  they never see the conversation above, only this rewrite — so an
  unresolved pronoun here becomes a search for "its pricing" with no "its"
  to find. Do not add, answer, or infer anything the question did not ask;
  only make its existing meaning explicit.
- "query_type": "simple_lookup" is a single well-known fact with one
  uncontroversial answer that does not benefit from multi-source research,
  comparison, or synthesis -- "who is X", "what is Y", a definition, a date, a

  recommendation, or analysis, and anything tied to this client's own
  engagement. When genuinely unsure, choose "research" -- underspending effort
  on a question that needed it is worse than a wasted research pass on a
  simple one. A question naming this client, its documents, its metrics, or
  asking to compare/evaluate/recommend is never "simple_lookup".
- "recommendation" when the user asks whether to adopt, choose, or invest in something.
- "comparison" when two or more options are weighed against each other.
- "quantitative" when the answer is a number, metric, or trend.
- "knowledge" otherwise.
- Always include "vector" and "bm25" unless the question names an exact identifier,
  code, or proper noun that favours lexical search alone.
- Add "analytics_sql" when the question asks for a measured metric, rate, count, or

- Concretely, add "needs_external" for: comparisons between named companies,
  products, platforms, or vendors; questions about industry standards,
  frameworks, or official documentation (e.g. OWASP, NIST, a cloud provider's

  entity. This controls whether the system checks that every named entity got
  a fair, comparably-sized amount of research before letting the answer
  declare one better than another.
- Set needs_decomposition true for comparisons, multi-part questions, or any question
